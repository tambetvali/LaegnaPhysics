The distance of 0.97 light-years does not belong to a physical gravitational body, but rather to an accelerating observer or spacecraft moving at a constant acceleration of 1 Earth gravity (\(1\text{ g}\) or \(9.81\text{ m/s}^2\)).

In special relativity, this boundary is known as the Rindler Horizon. If a rocket accelerates indefinitely at a comfortable \(1\text{ g}\) to simulate Earth's gravity for its passengers, an artificial event horizon forms exactly 0.97 light-years directly behind the ship. Because the rocket’s speed continuously climbs closer and closer to the speed of light, any light signal or object crossing that threshold from behind can never catch up to the ship for as long as it maintains its acceleration.

## 2. The Maximum Distance Paradox: Photons vs. Space Expansion

### The Photon's Perspective (Proper Time Lifetime)
From the frame of reference of a light particle (photon), **time does not pass**. 
* Due to infinite time dilation at the speed of light ($v = c$), a photon's proper lifetime ($\tau$) is exactly **zero**.
* Simultaneously, length contraction reduces the entire universe along its path to a flat plane of **zero thickness**.
* Therefore, in its own "linear-time lifetime," a photon experiences its creation, its journey across the universe, and its destruction at the exact same instantaneous moment. 

### The Coordinate Perspective (Maximum Distance of Space Overall)
When evaluating the maximum physical distance space allows *any* light particle to traverse in this distance function, we must use coordinate time from an external perspective. This reveals a strict cosmological ceiling.

- **The Absolute Limit:** **~62 Billion Light-Years**
- **The Mechanism:** This is the **Future Comoving Horizon**. It represents the absolute maximum distance a photon emitted *today* (or at any point in the future) can ever travel through space before the accelerated expansion of the universe separates the destination from the photon permanently. 
- **The Result:** Even if a photon travels eternally across cosmic time ($t \to \infty$), the flat speed function progressively reduces its effective progress relative to the expanding grid of space. Once it hits that 62-billion-light-year threshold, the expansion rate exactly equals the speed of light, freezing its forward progress relative to the distant universe.
